A Technical & Practical Guide (2014–2026)

The Evolution of Attention Mechanisms

How neural attention evolved from early translation alignment to modern foundation models like DeepSeek-V4, Kimi K3, and Qwen 3.8. Designed to be simple and intuitive at first look, with expandable technical deep-dives and exact mathematical formulations for every paper.

Four Primary Engineering Vectors
Exact & Positional

Preserves full pairwise visibility while making computation faster on GPUs via memory tiling or coordinate rotations.

KV & Recurrence

Compresses the growing key-value memory cache during generation or reformulates attention as fast linear state updates.

Context Scaling

Adapts positional frequencies so models can process sequences far longer than their original training limit without retraining from scratch.

Modern Frontiers

Blends linear recurrence layers, sparse routing networks, and latent compression into high-throughput hybrid architectures.

01 Sep 2014 exact

Bahdanau Additive Attention

Let the decoder dynamically search across all encoder words instead of squashing sentences into a single fixed vector.

What existed

Early sequence-to-sequence translation models squeezed an entire source sentence into a single, fixed-size hidden vector at the end of an RNN encoder.

The problem

Long sentences exceeded the memory capacity of one vector. The model forgot words from the beginning of the sentence by the time it reached the end.

How and why it works

Instead of discarding intermediate encoder steps, the decoder keeps all encoder hidden states. At each output step, it computes a dynamic similarity score against every source word using a small feed-forward network, creating a custom blended context vector for each translated word.

Additive alignment: Softmax weights spread dynamically across source tokens.

What you get

  • Solves the memory bottleneck on long sentences by giving the model direct access to all source tokens.
  • Improves translation quality significantly across complex sentences.

What you give up

  • Runs sequentially inside a recurrent loop, preventing parallel training across time steps.
  • Computing alignment scores for every word pair takes quadratic time as sentences grow.

Use it when

Translating sequences where dynamic source alignment is required across variable-length inputs.

Technical Deep-Dive & Mathematical Formulation

Alignment Formula

The alignment score \(e_{ij}\) between decoder state \(s_{i-1}\) and encoder state \(h_j\) is computed via a learned feed-forward layer with tanh activation:

\[e_{ij} = v_a^\top \tanh(W_a s_{i-1} + U_a h_j)\]

Context Vector Generation

Scores are normalized via softmax across source length \(T_x\) to produce attention weights \(\alpha_{ij}\), and the context vector \(c_i\) is the weighted sum:

\[\alpha_{ij} = \frac{\exp(e_{ij})}{\sum_{k=1}^{T_x} \exp(e_{ik})}, \quad c_i = \sum_{j=1}^{T_x} \alpha_{ij} h_j\]

Complexity: \(\mathcal{O}(T_x T_y)\) sequential evaluations, where \(T_x\) and \(T_y\) are source and target sequence lengths.

Bahdanau, Cho & Bengio · Neural Machine Translation by Jointly Learning to Align and TranslatearXiv:1409.0473 · submitted 01 Sep 2014

17 Aug 2015 exact

Luong Multiplicative Attention

Speed up attention scoring using direct vector dot products and focus compute using local Gaussian search windows.

What existed

Bahdanau's additive attention required passing every state pair through a separate neural network layer, adding computational overhead.

The problem

Evaluating full attention across long documents was slow, and global attention forced models to inspect every word even when translating short local phrases.

How and why it works

Luong simplified scoring into direct matrix multiplications (dot products) that run much faster on hardware. He also introduced local attention, which centers a small window over the most relevant source position and applies a Gaussian penalty outside that window.

Luong multiplicative scoring: Bilinear dot products with Gaussian window bounds.

What you get

  • Matrix dot products run significantly faster on GPUs than additive multi-layer networks.
  • Local windowing avoids paying the cost of global attention across long passages.

What you give up

  • Still operates inside sequential recurrent loops, preventing parallel execution across sequence length.
  • Dot products without variance scaling become unstable in high dimensions.

Use it when

Fast sequence-to-sequence translation models with local target alignments.

Technical Deep-Dive & Mathematical Formulation

Score Formulations

Luong introduced three score functions comparing current decoder state \(s_t\) with source state \(h_s\):

\[\operatorname{score}(s_t, h_s) = \begin{cases} s_t^\top h_s & \text{dot} \\ s_t^\top W_a h_s & \text{general} \\ v_a^\top \tanh(W_a [s_t; h_s]) & \text{concat} \end{cases}\]

Local Attention Windowing

In local attention, the model predicts an alignment position \(p_t\) and constrains attention to \([p_t - D, p_t + D]\) with Gaussian weighting:

\[p_t = S \cdot \operatorname{sigmoid}(v_p^\top \tanh(W_p s_t))\] \[a_t(s) = \operatorname{align}(s_t, h_s) \exp\left(-\frac{(s - p_t)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)\]

Luong, Pham & Manning · Effective Approaches to Attention-based Neural Machine TranslationarXiv:1508.04025 · submitted 17 Aug 2015

12 Jun 2017 exact

Scaled Dot-Product & Multi-Head Attention

Eliminate sequential recurrence completely by computing all pairwise token relationships in parallel across multiple independent feature heads.

What existed

Recurrent networks (RNNs, LSTMs) processed text word-by-word in sequence, preventing GPUs from processing entire sentences simultaneously.

The problem

Sequential execution severely bottlenecked training throughput, and unscaled dot products caused softmax gradients to vanish in high dimensions.

How and why it works

Vaswani et al. replaced recurrence with pure self-attention: every token projects into Query, Key, and Value vectors. Attention is computed across all token pairs at once using matrix multiplication scaled by \(1/\sqrt{d_k}\). Splitting vectors across multiple parallel heads allows the model to track grammar, subject-verb links, and factual associations independently.

Dense causal lower-triangular attention matrix: Every token attends to all previous tokens.

What you get

  • Full parallel training across sequence length on GPUs.
  • Multiple heads capture distinct syntactic and semantic relationships at the same time.

What you give up

  • Prefill compute and memory scale quadratically with sequence length (\(\mathcal{O}(L^2)\)).
  • Autoregressive generation requires storing past Keys and Values in memory.

Use it when

Standard foundation model architecture for high-capacity language modeling.

Technical Deep-Dive & Mathematical Formulation

Scaled Dot-Product Equation

\[\operatorname{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \operatorname{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V\]

where \(Q, K, V \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d_k}\). The scaling factor \(1/\sqrt{d_k}\) prevents large dot products from saturating softmax gradients.

Multi-Head Projection

\[\operatorname{MultiHead}(Q, K, V) = \operatorname{Concat}(\text{head}_1, \dots, \text{head}_H) W^O\] \[\text{where } \text{head}_i = \operatorname{Attention}(Q W_i^Q, K W_i^K, V W_i^V)\]

Complexity: \(2L^2 d\) FLOPs per layer during prefill; \(\mathcal{O}(L)\) memory bandwidth per generated token during decoding.

Vaswani et al. · Attention Is All You NeedarXiv:1706.03762 · submitted 12 Jun 2017

12 Jun 2017 position

Sinusoidal & Learned Positional Encodings

Add geometric coordinate signals directly to token embeddings so order-blind attention understands word sequences.

What existed

Recurrent networks tracked order naturally through step-by-step unrolling. Self-attention, however, is permutation-equivariant (it treats words like an unordered bag of tokens).

The problem

Without positional information, 'dog bites man' and 'man bites dog' produce identical attention scores.

How and why it works

Vaswani et al. added coordinate waves to each word vector before the first layer. Using sinusoidal functions of varying wavelengths, every position receives a unique geometric fingerprint, allowing the network to easily calculate relative distance between any two tokens.

What you get

  • Gives self-attention precise sequence order awareness with zero extra parameters for sinusoidal encodings.
  • Sinusoidal waves mathematically enable simple linear shifts across positions.

What you give up

  • Adding coordinate numbers directly into word vectors contaminates pure semantic word meanings.
  • Fails to generalize cleanly when evaluated on sequences longer than the training window.

Use it when

Standard baseline positional encoding for classical Transformer architectures.

Technical Deep-Dive & Mathematical Formulation

Sinusoidal Formulas

\[PE_{(pos, 2i)} = \sin\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d}}\right), \quad PE_{(pos, 2i+1)} = \cos\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d}}\right)\]

For any fixed offset \(k\), \(PE_{pos+k}\) can be represented as a linear transformation of \(PE_{pos}\) via rotation matrix multiplication.

Learned Positional Embeddings

Alternatively, allocate a lookup table \(E_{\text{pos}} \in \mathbb{R}^{L_{\max} \times d}\) where vector \(e_p\) is learned via gradient descent. This hard-caps sequence length at \(L_{\max}\).

Vaswani et al. · Attention Is All You NeedarXiv:1706.03762 · submitted 12 Jun 2017

06 Mar 2018 position

Shaw Relative Positional Representations

Inject relative distance vectors into attention logits rather than memorizing absolute coordinate numbers.

What existed

Transformers added absolute position numbers to input embeddings, forcing models to memorize exact coordinate indexes.

The problem

Language syntax depends on relative word spacing (e.g. '3 words apart') rather than absolute page positions (e.g. 'token 412').

How and why it works

Shaw et al. injected learned relative distance embeddings directly into the attention scoring and value mixing formulas. Distances beyond a maximum range \(k\) are clipped to a single maximum distance vector.

What you get

  • Models generalize better across varying sequence lengths because scores depend on relative offsets.
  • Reduces the need to memorize absolute coordinate indices.

What you give up

  • Adds tensor broadcasting overhead during logit computation on GPUs.
  • Cannot differentiate distances that fall beyond the clipping horizon \(k\).

Use it when

Language understanding and translation tasks where relative distance matters more than absolute index.

Technical Deep-Dive & Mathematical Formulation

Logit and Value Injection

\[e_{ij} = \frac{x_i W^Q (x_j W^K + a_{ij}^K)^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\] \[z_i = \sum_{j=1}^n \alpha_{ij} (x_j W^V + a_{ij}^V)\]

Clipping Mechanism

\[a_{ij}^K = w_{\operatorname{clip}(j-i, k)}^K, \quad \operatorname{clip}(x, k) = \max(-k, \min(k, x))\]

Learns \(2k + 1\) relative vectors \(w_{-k}^K, \dots, w_k^K\), constraining relative parameter overhead.

Shaw, Uszkoreit & Vaswani · Self-Attention with Relative Position RepresentationsarXiv:1803.02155 · submitted 06 Mar 2018

23 Apr 2019 sparse

Sparse Transformer

Cut quadratic attention compute down to square-root scaling by factorizing attention into local and periodic strided steps.

What existed

Standard self-attention calculated all pairwise interactions between tokens, requiring quadratic compute that was too slow for long sequences.

The problem

Processing long audio signals, high-resolution images, or long text documents stalled GPU memory during training.

How and why it works

Child et al. factorized the attention matrix into two simple geometric patterns: a local sliding window for nearby context, and periodic strided steps (jumping every few tokens) to cover distant tokens in two hops, reducing complexity from quadratic to sub-quadratic.

Factorized attention: Local windows combined with periodic strided vertical bars.

What you get

  • Cuts compute and memory scaling from \(\mathcal{O}(L^2)\) down to \(\mathcal{O}(L\sqrt{L})\).
  • Enables processing sequences of up to 8,000 tokens on 2019 GPUs.

What you give up

  • Fixed geometric patterns cannot adapt dynamically to data content.
  • Requires custom block-sparse GPU kernels to achieve real hardware acceleration.

Use it when

Long-sequence generative modeling on images, audio waveforms, or structured text.

Technical Deep-Dive & Mathematical Formulation

Factorized Index Sets

Decomposes full attention into two subsets \(S_i = S_i^{(1)} \cup S_i^{(2)}\):

\[S_i^{(1)} = \{j : \lfloor j/p \rfloor = \lfloor i/p \rfloor\} \quad (\text{local block})\] \[S_i^{(2)} = \{j : (i - j) \bmod p = 0\} \quad (\text{strided step})\]

Choosing stride \(p \approx \sqrt{L}\) reduces total active attention connections from \(L^2\) to \(L\sqrt{L}\).

Child, Gray, Radford & Sutskever · Generating Long Sequences with Sparse TransformersarXiv:1904.10509 · submitted 23 Apr 2019

23 Oct 2019 position

T5 Relative Position Bias

Add simple learnable scalar offsets to attention logits with logarithmic bucketing for long-distance offsets.

What existed

Shaw relative embeddings added high-dimensional vectors to Keys and Values, increasing memory footprint and tensor operations.

The problem

Models needed a simpler, lightweight way to inject distance awareness without bloating projection matrices or compute overhead.

How and why it works

Raffel et al. added a simple learnable scalar number directly to the attention logit. To handle long distances efficiently, immediate neighbor distances get individual numbers, while distant offsets share logarithmically spaced buckets.

What you get

  • Minimal parameter overhead (just 32 learnable scalar bias numbers per head).
  • Logarithmic binning handles long sequence offsets cleanly.

What you give up

  • Does not compress the key-value cache or reduce quadratic prefill compute.
  • Cannot extrapolate beyond its maximum distance bucket.

Use it when

Encoder-decoder models and standard NLP sequence-to-sequence tasks (T5, ByT5).

Technical Deep-Dive & Mathematical Formulation

Logit Bias Formula

\[A_{ij} = \frac{q_i k_j^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}} + b_{\operatorname{bucket}(j - i)}\]

Logarithmic Distance Binning

For offset \(d = j - i\), maps distances into 32 buckets:

\[\operatorname{bucket}(d) = \begin{cases} d & \text{if } d < 16 \\ 16 + \left\lfloor \frac{\log(d / 16)}{\log(128 / 16)} \times 16 \right\rfloor & \text{if } 16 \le d < 128 \\ 31 & \text{if } d \ge 128 \end{cases}\]

Raffel et al. · Exploring the Limits of Transfer Learning with a Unified Text-to-Text TransformerarXiv:1910.10683 · submitted 23 Oct 2019

Multi-Query Attention (MQA)

Force all query heads to share a single key head and value head to slash memory bandwidth bottleneck during generation.

What existed

Multi-Head Attention created separate Key and Value vectors for every query head (e.g. 32 heads = 32 Key and 32 Value heads).

The problem

During text generation, fetching 32 separate Key/Value heads from slow GPU memory for every generated token created a massive memory bandwidth stall.

How and why it works

Noam Shazeer proposed Multi-Query Attention: keep multiple Query heads for expressive reasoning, but share exactly 1 Key head and 1 Value head across all of them.

MQA architecture: Multiple query heads collapse into 1 shared key and 1 shared value head.

What you get

  • Reduces KV cache memory size and GPU memory bandwidth reads by \(H_Q\times\) (e.g. \(32\times\)).
  • Significantly increases decoding throughput and serving batch sizes.

What you give up

  • Slightly degrades model capacity and associative recall on complex reasoning benchmarks.
  • Requires training from scratch or specialized checkpoint adaptation.

Use it when

High-throughput real-time text generation where serving cost and memory bandwidth are primary constraints (PaLM, StarCoder).

Technical Deep-Dive & Mathematical Formulation

Head Dimensionality

\[Q \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times H_Q \times L \times d_k}, \quad K \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 1 \times L \times d_k}, \quad V \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 1 \times L \times d_k}\]

Decode Memory Bandwidth

Per-token memory traffic during decoding drops from \(2 \cdot B \cdot L \cdot H_Q \cdot d_k\) down to \(2 \cdot B \cdot L \cdot d_k\), unlocking \(H_Q\times\) larger serving batch sizes in GPU SRAM/HBM pipelines.

Noam Shazeer · Fast Transformer Decoding: One Write-Head is All You NeedarXiv:1911.02150 · submitted 05 Nov 2019

13 Jan 2020 sparse

Reformer (LSH Attention)

Use Locality-Sensitive Hashing to compute attention only between similar tokens, and reversible layers to eliminate backprop activation memory.

What existed

Full attention computed dot products between all token pairs, even though most distant pairs receive near-zero attention weights.

The problem

Storing intermediate activation matrices across layers for backpropagation consumed gigabytes of GPU memory.

How and why it works

Reformer hashes queries and keys into angular buckets using Locality-Sensitive Hashing (LSH) and only calculates attention between tokens in the same bucket. It also uses reversible residual layers to reconstruct activations on-the-fly during backpropagation.

What you get

  • Reduces attention compute from \(\mathcal{O}(L^2)\) to \(\mathcal{O}(L \log L)\).
  • Reversible layers eliminate storing layer activations, drastically reducing training memory.

What you give up

  • Stochastic hashing can randomly miss relevant tokens in different buckets.
  • Irregular bucket sizes lead to lower GPU Tensor Core utilization.

Use it when

Memory-constrained training on extremely long input sequences.

Technical Deep-Dive & Mathematical Formulation

LSH Angular Hashing

\[h(x) = \operatorname{arg\,max}([xR; -xR])\]

where \(R \in \mathbb{R}^{d_k \times b/2}\) is a random projection matrix. Tokens are sorted by hash bucket and split into chunks of size \(2B\).

Reversible Residual Layers

\[y_1 = x_1 + \operatorname{Attention}(x_2), \quad y_2 = x_2 + \operatorname{FeedForward}(y_1)\] \[x_2 = y_2 - \operatorname{FeedForward}(y_1), \quad x_1 = y_1 - \operatorname{Attention}(x_2)\]

Kitaev, Kaiser & Levskaya · Reformer: The Efficient TransformerarXiv:2001.04451 · submitted 13 Jan 2020

12 Mar 2020 sparse

Routing Transformer

Cluster tokens dynamically on a hypersphere using spherical k-means to compute attention strictly within matching clusters.

What existed

Locality-Sensitive Hashing in Reformer used random hyperplanes, which often split semantically related tokens into separate buckets.

The problem

Random hashing buckets introduced noise and missed important long-range associations.

How and why it works

Routing Transformer uses online spherical k-means clustering to group queries and keys into semantic clusters on a unit hypersphere, computing attention only between tokens that belong to the same cluster.

What you get

  • Reduces compute complexity to \(\mathcal{O}(L\sqrt{L})\) with higher retrieval accuracy than random hashing.
  • Learns data-dependent semantic clusters dynamically during training.

What you give up

  • Online cluster centroid updates add GPU synchronization stalls.
  • Hard discrete cluster assignments can create non-differentiable routing boundaries.

Use it when

Long-sequence modeling where semantic clustering outperforms fixed geometric sliding windows.

Technical Deep-Dive & Mathematical Formulation

Spherical k-Means Routing

Projects normalized vectors \(\tilde{x} = x / \|x\|_2\) to cluster centroids \(\mu_k\):

\[c_i = \operatorname{arg\,max}_{k \in [1, K]} (\tilde{x}_i \cdot \mu_k), \quad \mu_k \leftarrow \operatorname{Normalize}\left(\sum_{i: c_i = k} \tilde{x}_i\right)\]

Each query attends only to keys sharing its top cluster centroid, reducing attention evaluations to \(\mathcal{O}(L^{1.5})\).

Roy, Saffar, Vaswani & Grangier · Efficient Content-Based Sparse Attention with Routing TransformersarXiv:2003.05997 · submitted 12 Mar 2020

10 Apr 2020 sparse

Longformer (Sliding Window Attention)

Combine local sliding windows with task-specific global tokens to process long documents with linear complexity.

What existed

Full self-attention scaled quadratically, preventing standard Transformers from reading documents longer than 512 tokens.

The problem

Real-world documents (contracts, books, research papers) span thousands of tokens, overflowing GPU memory under full attention.

How and why it works

Longformer combines three practical attention patterns: a local sliding window (e.g. 512 tokens) for immediate context, dilated gaps for broader context, and designated global tokens (like the question or title) that can attend to and be attended by all tokens.

Sliding window attention band with full horizontal/vertical crossbars for global tokens.

What you get

  • Linear scaling \(\mathcal{O}(L \cdot w)\) in compute and memory with document length.
  • Standard architecture for long-document question answering and classification.

What you give up

  • Requires manually choosing which tokens receive global attention.
  • Tokens outside the local window require multiple layers to propagate information.

Use it when

Long-document classification, question answering, and document extraction tasks.

Technical Deep-Dive & Mathematical Formulation

Pattern Composition

Constructs attention mask \(M_{ij}\) combining three index sets:

\[M_{ij} = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } |i - j| \le w/2 \quad (\text{sliding window}) \\ 0 & \text{if } (i - j) \bmod d = 0, |i - j| \le d \cdot w/2 \quad (\text{dilated}) \\ 0 & \text{if } i \in G \text{ or } j \in G \quad (\text{global tokens}) \\ -\infty & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}\]

Dedicated projection matrices \(W_Q^g, W_K^g, W_V^g\) are used for global attention heads.

Beltagy, Peters & Cohan · Longformer: The Long-Document TransformerarXiv:2004.05150 · submitted 10 Apr 2020

08 Jun 2020 linear

Linformer

Project key and value sequence dimensions down to a small fixed rank to achieve linear time and memory attention.

What existed

Attention calculated an \(L \times L\) matrix comparing every query against every key.

The problem

Storing the full \(L \times L\) attention matrix was too expensive for long sequences, even though the matrix has low theoretical rank.

How and why it works

Wang et al. proved mathematically that the attention score matrix is low-rank. Linformer projects the sequence length of Keys and Values from \(L\) down to a small fixed dimension \(k\) (e.g. 256) using simple linear projection matrices, making attention compute linear in \(L\).

What you get

  • Strict \(\mathcal{O}(L \cdot k)\) linear time and memory complexity.
  • Simple implementation using standard linear layer projections.

What you give up

  • Fixed projection dimension \(k\) limits resolution on fine-grained associative lookups.
  • Cannot be applied straightforwardly to causal autoregressive generation.

Use it when

Bidirectional encoder models (like BERT) processing fixed-length document classifications.

Technical Deep-Dive & Mathematical Formulation

Low-Rank Sequence Projections

Projects \(K, V \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d}\) to rank \(k\) using projection matrices \(E, F \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times L}\):

\[\tilde{K} = E K \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times d}, \quad \tilde{V} = F V \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times d}\] \[\operatorname{Linformer}(Q, K, V) = \operatorname{softmax}\left(\frac{Q \tilde{K}^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) \tilde{V}\]

Complexity: \(\mathcal{O}(L \cdot k \cdot d)\) compute and memory.

Wang et al. · Linformer: Self-Attention with Linear ComplexityarXiv:2006.04768 · submitted 08 Jun 2020

29 Jun 2020 linear

Linear Attention via Kernel Feature Maps

Reorder matrix multiplications using kernel feature maps to turn quadratic attention into constant-memory linear recurrence.

What existed

Softmax placed Queries and Keys inside an exponential function before multiplying by Values, locking computation into quadratic order.

The problem

Autoregressive generation required growing the key-value cache linearly with every step, consuming gigabytes of memory.

How and why it works

Katharopoulos et al. replaced softmax with kernel feature maps \(\phi(x)\). By using the associative property of matrix multiplication, Keys and Values can be multiplied first into a running state matrix, turning attention into a fast linear-time recurrent system with constant decoding memory.

Recurrent linear attention: Fixed-size matrix accumulator updated token-by-token.

What you get

  • Linear \(\mathcal{O}(L)\) prefill training time on GPUs.
  • Constant \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) memory and time per step during autoregressive decoding.

What you give up

  • Ungated additive updates saturate the memory state over long sequences.
  • Lacks the sharp exponential retrieval focus of true softmax attention.

Use it when

High-throughput real-time streaming, edge inference, and audio generation.

Technical Deep-Dive & Mathematical Formulation

Associative Kernel Reordering

Using feature map \(\phi(x) = \operatorname{elu}(x) + 1\):

\[\operatorname{Attention}(Q, K, V)_i = \frac{\phi(q_i)^\top \sum_{j=1}^i \phi(k_j) v_j^\top}{\phi(q_i)^\top \sum_{j=1}^i \phi(k_j)}\]

Recurrent State Formulation

\[S_t = S_{t-1} + \phi(k_t) v_t^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{d_k \times d_v}, \quad z_t = z_{t-1} + \phi(k_t) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_k}\] \[y_t = \frac{\phi(q_t)^\top S_t}{\phi(q_t)^\top z_t}\]

Katharopoulos, Vyas, Yumer & Mathieu · Transformers are RNNs: Fast Autoregressive Transformers with Linear AttentionarXiv:2006.16236 · submitted 29 Jun 2020

28 Jul 2020 sparse

BigBird

Prove that combining random connections, sliding windows, and global tokens preserves universal sequence approximation in linear time.

What existed

Sparse attention methods reduced compute by dropping connections, but lacked theoretical guarantees on whether the model lost expressiveness.

The problem

Engineers were unsure if sparse attention models could still solve any sequence task that full Transformers could.

How and why it works

Zaheer et al. proved mathematically that combining three simple sparse connections—random connections, local sliding windows, and global tokens—creates a graph that preserves all theoretical properties of full self-attention (universal approximation and Turing completeness) with linear complexity.

BigBird sparse graph: Local window + random edges + global token crossbars.

What you get

  • Linear \(\mathcal{O}(L)\) complexity backed by rigorous mathematical proofs.
  • Enables 8x longer sequence lengths on genomics, summarization, and QA.

What you give up

  • Random sparse memory gathering causes non-contiguous memory access on GPUs.
  • Complex implementation for causal autoregressive decoding.

Use it when

Genomic sequence modeling, long scientific document summarization, and large document QA.

Technical Deep-Dive & Mathematical Formulation

Sparse Graph Definition

The BigBird attention graph \(G = (V, E)\) connects token \(i\) to three edge sets:

\[E_i = \{j : |i - j| \le w\} \cup \{j : j \in \operatorname{Random}(r)\} \cup \{j : j \le g \text{ or } i \le g\}\]

Total node degree is \(d = 2w + r + 2g \ll L\), yielding strict \(\mathcal{O}(L)\) FLOP complexity.

Zaheer et al. · Big Bird: Transformers for Longer SequencesarXiv:2007.14062 · submitted 28 Jul 2020

21 Sep 2020 linear

Performer (FAVOR+)

Approximate exact softmax attention unbiasedly using Positive Orthogonal Random Features without changing model weights.

What existed

Early kernel approximations often produced negative values, causing unstable division by zero and training crashes.

The problem

Approximating true exponential softmax attention with standard random features required huge sample sizes to avoid high variance.

How and why it works

Choromanski et al. introduced Fast Attention Via Positive Orthogonal Random Features (FAVOR+): by enforcing positive exponential feature maps and orthogonal random vectors, Performer provides an unbiased, low-variance approximation of softmax in linear time.

What you get

  • Linear \(\mathcal{O}(L \cdot M)\) compute and memory scaling.

Hardware Constraints and Architecture Selection

Attention architecture selection is determined by two hardware bottlenecks: compute-bound prefill (FLOP limits) and bandwidth-bound decoding (HBM transfer limits):

Prefill Bottleneck (Compute-Bound)

Processing prompt length \(L\) requires \(2L^2 d_k\) FLOPs. On GPUs, Tensor Core saturation is maximized via IO-aware SRAM tiling (FlashAttention-1/2/3). For \(L \ge 64\text{k}\), compute scaling mandates structural sparsity (NSA, DSA, DeepSeek-V4 CSA) or linear recurrence (DeltaNet, KDA, GLA).

Decode Bottleneck (Memory-Bound)

Generating token-by-token requires loading the entire KV cache from HBM to SRAM for arithmetic intensity \(\approx 1\) FLOP/byte. Serving throughput is directly proportional to KV cache compression (MQA, GQA, CLA, MLA).

Context Extrapolation Bottleneck (Geometry-Bound)

Evaluating sequences beyond training bounds causes positional distribution shifts. Additive coordinates fail; rotational geometry requires frequency-banded scaling (YaRN, LongRoPE, M-RoPE) or complete positional dropping (DroPE).
